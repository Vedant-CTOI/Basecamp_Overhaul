# Client Deliverable Framing — Analysis Report Structure

*Origin: developed during the Coke LA28 workshop synthesis report (March 2026). Was held in `memory/feedback-client-deliverables.md`; moved here 2026-05-26 as the default structure for any future workshop-platform engagement's client report.*

## Lead with higher-order patterns, not team-by-team breakdowns

**Why:** A client reading a workshop report doesn't care about how each team used the tool differently. They care about what patterns emerged across teams (things only visible when looking at all data simultaneously), what conflicts or dependencies need attention, and what gaps nobody touched. Team-by-team breakdowns read like a category manager's inventory — the interesting stuff is the cross-team convergences, divergences, and flags.

## Default structure

1. **At a Glance** — headline stats + top ideas (1 page max)
2. **Cross-Team Patterns** — convergences and what they signal (core of the report)
3. **Conflicts & Dependencies** — competing claims, shared needs, early conversations
4. **Gaps** — what nobody touched that someone should
5. **Interesting Kernels** — distinctive underdeveloped ideas worth a second look
6. **Team Portfolios** — brief appendix, not the main event

## Voice

- No AI writing tics. No jargon, dramatizing, or wittiness.
- Should read like an expert analyst and product manager.
- Where interpreting data, **say so**. Gaps are welcome.
- Use `text-wrap: pretty` and `balance` for headings to prevent orphaned words.

## When to apply

Default structure for any workshop-platform engagement synthesis report. If a specific engagement has unusual structure (e.g., a single-team deep-dive, or a positioning workshop with no teams), adapt — but the *spirit* (pattern-first, not inventory-first) holds across shapes.
