# Team Touffou activation image prompts

These five prompts generated the presentation-ready activation frames in
`public/activation-images/team-touffou/`. They were run as separate images
with the built-in image generation tool.

Shared direction: photorealistic 16:9 key visuals, plausible enough for a
client activation deck, restrained Ogilvy red, no logos, no readable copy,
no trademarks, no watermarks, and no text overlays.

## What these frames taught the standing prompt

The useful common denominator is one **focal proposition**, not one literal
subject. The Almanac can be carried by a single artifact; the other ideas need
an activation mechanism, the people using it, and enough environmental context
to explain the proposal in one frame. The standing prompt in
`app/lib/config.ts` now preserves these traits:

- Show the concrete proof of the idea — artifact, service encounter, space, or
  decisive human moment — rather than a symbolic image of its theme.
- Let people, props, and embedded technology clarify how the idea works, but
  give every supporting element a job.
- Make production design buildable and particular: credible materials, scale,
  practical details, and a setting chosen by the idea rather than a default
  dark agency room.
- Use one dominant motivated light with plausible practicals. “Exactly one
  light source” is too rigid for a real activation environment.
- Treat red as a disciplined family of related accents, as in the Vault's
  drawers or the Homework Engine's workbench, rather than allowing only one
  isolated red pixel or flooding the scene with red atmosphere.
- Ban readable generated copy while allowing abstract charts, image grids,
  china-marker annotations, and screens that are physically necessary to make
  the mechanism legible.
- Keep the photography crisp and material without applying a fake halftone
  filter over the scene. The image should reproduce well on press; it should
  not look like a photograph of a printout.

## Do the Homework Engine

Bring "Do the Homework Engine" to life as a premium, believable agency
activation. An overnight intelligence engine digests decades of category
history, cultural signals, and a client's creative archive. At dawn, four
senior strategists and creatives review the resulting field guide before a
brainstorm. Set the scene in a cinematic modern agency war room with a long,
well-organized evidence wall, an elegant central workstation, cool sunrise
light, and restrained red practical light. The technology should support the
human decision rather than dominate it. Avoid holograms, science-fiction
interfaces, and generic neon-tech styling.

## The Craft Vault

Bring "The Craft Vault" to life as a working archive the whole agency can
raid. Create a believable hybrid archive where floor-to-ceiling flat files,
red drawers, original mechanicals, copy decks, treatments, and killed
campaigns meet a central digitization table and restrained search screens.
Show a mixed-generation creative team handling original boards while a
colleague searches the index and places a relevant precedent beside a live
brief. The feeling should be tactile, active, culturally rich, and useful,
not dusty nostalgia, a fantasy library, or a server room.

## The Category Almanac

Bring "The Category Almanac" to life as a numbered annual intelligence
product clients would pay to keep. Photograph a premium red clothbound
volume open on a senior client's working table, with analytical photography,
charts, marginal notes, and tabbed sections. Place a numbered slipcase and
three slim quarterly update booklets nearby, with past editions on a small
shelf behind. Treat it as an authoritative consulting-grade publication,
not a decorative coffee-table book. Use abstract lines and charts instead of
readable copy.

## The Second Opinion

Bring "The Second Opinion" to life as a bookable 48-hour campaign clinic.
Create a highly designed glass-fronted review room where a brand has brought
live work into market. A senior multidisciplinary trio returns what to
protect, what to change this week, and the opportunity the campaign missed.
Anchor the scene on one large unbranded campaign image with precise red
china-marker annotations, three physical verdict cards, and a restrained
countdown clock. The exchange with two client leaders should feel candid,
useful, and generous. Avoid medical parody, courtroom imagery, and hostile
critique.

## The Apprentice Deck

Bring "The Apprentice Deck" to life as visible apprenticeship on real work.
Set the scene in a consequential client presentation where a confident
early-career creative owns one section and presents it beside a seated senior
creative who is clearly giving them the floor. Show engaged client leaders,
marked-up drafts, and a large screen carrying the same unbranded campaign
visual. The image should feel candid, credible, and quietly confident, not a
classroom, a handshake photograph, or tokenistic staging.
