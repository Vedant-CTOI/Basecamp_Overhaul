# Basecamp — Ogilvy Showcase Edition: Design Direction

The implementation contract for the `ogilvy-generic` branch. Synthesized from four research streams: Ogilvy brand identity (verified against ogilvy.com CSS + official assets), an S-tier design inspiration sweep, the Coke LA28 teardown, and a full copy/asset inventory (~250 strings).

## Thesis

Basecamp dressed as it should be: **an advertising agency's instrument, not a software company's app.** The identity is typography, red, and the vernacular of advertising production — copy decks, proof sheets, selects, the overnight edition. Liveness borrows from the election desk, not sports TV: returns coming in, a needle settling, a morning paper. Every screen is in **platform showcase mode**: it explains what it is and what it's for, in the long-copy instructional tradition David Ogilvy invented.

## The three registers (from the Coke teardown — keep this inversion)

1. **Theater (dark)** — landing, team select, unlock, Stage, Big Board, **and the Operator Console with its login door** (Round 21). Near-black ground, white type, red as the focal voice.
2. **Workbench (light)** — team boards, quick-add, coaching. Paper ground, ink type, red flood band carries the brand.
3. **The Edition (print)** — report. Broadsheet conventions: masthead, dateline, column rules.

Phones (vote, quick-add) are light — a paper ballot is the least SaaS object there is.

## Type system (the real fonts — already in `app/public/fonts/`)

```
Ogilvy Serif  (Regular/Italic/Bold woff2)  — display, headlines, big numerals, editorial body
Ogilvy Sans   (Light/Regular/Italic/Bold)  — UI, labels, buttons, body at laptop scale
Courier Prime (Google Fonts)               — coach copy decks + production slugs ONLY
```

- Big room-facing numerals are set in **Ogilvy Serif** (Baskerville figures = election desk, not scoreboard). Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` where numbers tick; if Serif lacks tabular figures, live-ticking numbers fall back to Ogilvy Sans tabular.
- Labels/eyebrows: Ogilvy Sans Bold, 12–13px, +2–3px tracking, uppercase — sparingly (slugs and stamps, not above every block).
- Display sizes: hero 72–110px Serif; stage headlines ≥64px; room-facing secondary ≥48px @1080p; anything the room reads ≥16px high-contrast (standing projector rule).
- Kill Bebas Neue + Roboto imports in `app/layout.tsx`; @font-face the woff2s with `font-display: swap`.

## Signature elements (each encodes something true)

1. **The Kruger bar** — solid `--red` bar, white Ogilvy Sans Bold. Used for: current presenter on Stage, leading idea during results, the selected ballot option. One per screen.
2. **The china-marker select** — a hand-weight red SVG circle/underline stroke that draws itself in ~300ms when a real action happens: a vote cast, an idea shortlisted, a coach flag. This is the product's signature human mark (Magnum contact-sheet vocabulary). Never decorative.
3. **The coupon** — the room-code entry is a dashed-border box, the response coupon of classic direct-response ads.
4. **The slug line** — small Courier Prime line of true data on cards: `TEAM 02 · C1 · 14:32 · 87 WORDS`. Nothing fake.
5. **Rubber stamps** — state badges (`COACHED`, `SHORTLISTED`, `SCOUTED`, `SET ASIDE`) as single-color bordered stamp marks, 1–2° rotation, applied only when the state is real.
6. **The wire** — the bottom ticker is the ONE continuously moving element in the system (ink band, red LIVE chip, white text). Everything else holds still.

## Motion rules (motion is an event, never a texture)

- State changes **cut** or swap once (150–250ms ease-out). Nothing fades up on scroll.
- Numbers **tick** (counting is information).
- Exactly **two choreographed peaks**: voting results reveal; the winner moment (red slab treatment, D&AD-pencil scarcity — no confetti).
- China-marker strokes draw on action. Coke's unlock ritual and medallion launch sequence are kept, restyled (they are the entry peak).
- Nothing moves while type is being read on stage. No parallax, no hover theater on projected surfaces.

## Round 22 — Decisions read; process whispers (settled 2026-08-07, user ruling)

User ruling, verbatim: **"when you select something for the stage, i think the gray 'on stage' star is pretty dim on the board cards."** The Board card's `★ STAGE` flag was `rgba(35,31,32,0.55)` — **3.72:1** on the card's white ground, at 10px, where type needs 4.5:1. It now sits at `rgba(35,31,32,0.78)` (**7.97:1**).

The general rule this settles, because the value alone would not survive the next card-anatomy pass: **the bottom row carries two kinds of thing at two volumes.** A *decision the team made* — this idea goes to the Stage — reads. A *process fact the system is reporting* — the darkroom is working, a sheet is ready, a develop stalled — whispers at 0.55 and is allowed to, because it is ambient and it goes away on its own. They had been rendered identically, which is why a team's own choice sat at the volume of a spinner.

What this does NOT change: the flag stays borderless and stays on the right, so the card anatomy holds (ONE stamp on the left; flags, not second stamps, on the right). And it is **not red.** The Board is not the Stage — a team can mark several ideas at once, a shortlisted card already carries the red 2px border, and Round 15 item 6 holds that exactly one Kruger exists in production and it is the focus position chip. Red here would put two or three on a single card and would mark a selection rather than the room's current object, which Round 7 item 1 forbids.

Judged on renders at 1600×1000, deviceScaleFactor 2, against the `COACHED` stamp beside it: 0.55 recedes, full ink makes the star heavy enough to compete with the boxed stamp, 0.78 reads as a peer without shouting.

## Round 21 — The console is theater-dark (settled 2026-08-05, user ruling)

User ruling, verbatim: **"whether this is light or dark doesn't matter too much to me. maybe dark like the team select screen?"** The Operator Console and its login door move to the theater register, and the register list above now names them. This **replaces the sweep's light-workbench judgement call** — the doc had assigned admin to no register at all, which is why the sweep had to guess, and `docs/open-items.md` had been carrying "Admin's register" as an open decision ever since. It is closed.

The console's ground, seam and type ramp are **taken from the team-select surface, not invented for it**: `#0A0A0C` (`BRAND.colors.surface0`) ground, `#1B1A1D` (`surface1`) cards, `rgba(255,255,255,0.14)` seams, `#A8A5A6` secondary — the same values `app/page.tsx` sets in its own header. What did NOT change is what the surface is: pre-session configuration, dense and quiet at laptop distance, never projected and never read across a room.

1. **Three of the light console's rulings invert rather than survive, and each keeps its argument with the ground swapped.**

   *The primary.* A console has a dozen save buttons and a dozen red buttons is wallpaper, which is why every SAVE was an ink slab. The dark converse is the **paper slab** — white ground, ink type, **16.3:1 measured**, 15.0:1 as rendered. Disabled slabs went 0.40 → **0.50** (5.2:1; at 0.40 they smear on this ground in a way they never did on paper).

   *Red.* This register forbids red running text, so every red the light console SPOKE in is now white type behind a red edge and a red spine: the unmet pre-flight detail, the NOT SAVED line, both export failures, and the login's wrong-password line. **Red keeps exactly three jobs here — the READY slab (the one filled red, and it exists only when nothing is unmet), the mark against an unmet pre-flight line, and the LIVE chip when voting is open.** The breaking-news auto toggle gave up its red fill, which was a fourth meaning wearing the same paint.

   *Fields.* **A `WELL` that reads as an input on paper does not invert into one.** The light field was a 2.5%-ink inset; its literal inversion is nothing at all, because `#0A0A0C` inside `#1B1A1D` measures **1.14:1**. The EDGE carries the affordance instead, sized to the 3:1 WCAG asks of a component boundary: `rgba(255,255,255,0.38)` = **3.06:1 against the panel outside it and 3.45:1 against the fill inside it**, which is why it reads from both sides. And `.console-field` in `globals.css` finally puts something behind the `focus:outline-none` that sixteen fields were setting with no replacement — focus is a change in WEIGHT (edge to 0.55 plus an inset line), never a colour this register has already spent.

2. **On dark, loudness is luminance, not hue.** An unmet pre-flight line used to be red type; it is now full white against met lines on the quiet ramp, with a red ▪ as the mark. Same for team and category names: **Round 7 item 5 holds here — no coloured display type on dark** — so `paperType()` is gone from the console entirely. Its whole job is to DARKEN a hue until it clears on paper, which on this ground is exactly backwards. The hue keeps the dot beside the label; the label is white.

3. **`darkMark()` (`lib/config.ts`) is the mark's rule, and it is deliberately NOT `paperType`'s twin.** The Sharpener's coach hue IS ink (`#231F20`), and ink is not a hue on a near-black card — its spine measured **1.06:1** and was simply not on the screen, while the other three read 4.4 / 5.8 / 1.5. A mark is not read, so it does not owe 4.5:1, and lifting every hue until it did would fork this palette away from the Stage, which ships cobalt and oxblood spines raw on purpose. **Exactly one hue cannot survive here and it is the one that is not a hue.** The rule already existed as `onDark` inside `CoachTakeover`; it is one definition now, read by both.

4. **A row-level `opacity` cannot tell decoration from the controls that act on it.** A paused wire message dimmed wholesale, and on this ground that multiplied through the already-quiet tokens beneath it: the timestamp landed at **2.6:1** and the delete control — the one you reach for on a paused row — at **3.1:1**. The state moved to where it belongs (hollow dot, message on the quiet ramp, a button that already reads Resume: three signals), the controls stay at full strength, and nothing on the row is under 4.5:1. **Standing rule: dim the thing that is quiet, never the container that holds its controls.**

5. **The door moves with the room.** `app/admin-login` was written in the workbench register *because the console was*; left light it would have been a white flash on the way in, and the body ground under this whole app is already the stage dark, so the light card was the odd one out. Same tokens, one card, still no ceremony.

6. **What was deliberately NOT converted.** The **QR tiles stay white with ink codes** — a code is a printed, scanned object and inverting it breaks readers and printers alike. The **Kruger's own contrast is untouched at 3.94:1** (white on `#EB3F43`), which is what it measures on the light register too: it is the doc's signature element on every surface, and forking it for one console would be a worse outcome than the number. The red ▪ mark reads **4.39:1** and is held to the 3:1 non-text floor, not the 4.5:1 type floor, on purpose.

7. **Measured, not eyeballed.** A Playwright pass composites each text node's real ground from its ancestor chain rather than assuming one; anything inside an `opacity` group is read from rendered PIXELS, because a computed-style walk double-counts those (it reports the disabled slab at 2.19:1 where the pixels are 5.22:1). **Corollary: a contrast harness that composites text over an already-dimmed ground is measuring a colour that is not on the screen.** After the pass, every type pair on all eleven tabs is ≥4.5:1 except the two named in item 6. The floors: white body 17.3–19.8:1, `#A8A5A6` secondary 7.1–8.1:1, the 0.55 label ramp 5.7–6.3:1 (the light console's `FAINT` was 0.38 ink ≈ 2.9:1, so this is a lift, not a loss), placeholders 4.6:1.

Corollary to the session's slot-hunting: **the first impression is above the configurability ceiling.**

## Round 19 — The wall fills across, not down (settled 2026-08-03, user ruling)

**Settled after two failed attempts, same day (user ruling, verbatim): "I want each button to be half of one column, and then the other two columns are filled with content."** The making pockets are not a band above the wall in any form. They sit INSIDE the wall, at the head of column one, holding exactly that column's width with each pocket half of it — so the other columns carry ideas BESIDE them rather than beneath them. See item 3 for the two attempts that failed and why.

User ruling, verbatim: **"I don't know if the way it fills the rows with ideas here makes any sense."** It didn't.

1. **The Board's wall is a real masonry, and it fills SHORTEST COLUMN FIRST.** The wall was CSS multi-column, and multicol balances by HEIGHT: the browser fills column one to the floor before it starts column two. With one tall printed frame in play that put the first idea alone above a large dead gap, the next two stacked in column two, the rest in column three — the numbers scattered and the wall read down-then-across. Every card is now handed, in board order, to whichever column is currently shortest, which is the standard masonry placement: **the top row reads in board order left to right, and no column trails a hole while its neighbour is full.** (That row reads `pockets · 01 · 02` rather than `01 · 02 · 03` once item 3 seats the pockets at the head of column one; the law is the order, not the seat.) Column count is unchanged at 1 / 2 / 3, at the same breakpoints. Cards keep their own content height — a printed frame is taller than a text card, which is the point.

2. **Real flex columns, not absolutely positioned cards.** The placement is computed in JS (`useMasonry` in `app/app/[team]/page.tsx`) but rendered as ordinary columns in normal flow, so a stale or missing measurement can only cost balance for one frame; it can never overlap the wall. One `ResizeObserver` per wall catches the classic bug — a height that changes AFTER first paint, when a print lands, a font swaps or a description grows under an edit — and a single rAF batches a burst of those into one re-place. The pass provably settles in one step rather than oscillating, because **a card's height cannot depend on which column holds it: every column is the same width.** A card nobody has measured yet is estimated at the wall's median height, so a realtime arrival lands somewhere sane instead of reshuffling the whole wall.

3. **The making pockets live IN the wall, at the head of column one, one column wide, each half of it. This supersedes Round 15 item 2, and it is where two earlier attempts landed.** The band itself is retired, not resized.

   *Attempt one — a 170px pocket one column wide, above the wall.* It spent two thirds of its own height on nothing, left the other two thirds of that row in white, and pushed every idea down the page. User ruling that killed it: "we want as many ideas visible… now we just have all this weird extra white space on the right side."

   *Attempt two — a shallow full-width bar, above the wall.* It wasted no width, but it ignored the column rhythm entirely ("that weird full width thing") and still spent a whole band of the page on two controls. Widening the band answered the white space and lost the grid; the answer was never a band's proportions.

   *What ships.* The pair is a `grid grid-cols-2` cell at the head of masonry column one, holding that column's full width — **124px** tall wherever an idea can sit beside it. The wall's placement seeds column one's running height with **MAKING_H + the 24px gutter**, and that seed is the whole mechanism: it is what sends the first ideas to the columns BESIDE the pockets instead of underneath them. At three columns the top row reads **pockets · №01 · №02** on one top edge, with №03 the first card under the pockets; at two it reads **pockets · №01**. Reading order still runs with the numbers, no column trails a hole, and the pair costs one column of one row rather than a band of the page. 3. ~~A develop is two beats, not a fade.~~ **Pictures arrive as printed fact — the develop-in is REMOVED** (amended 2026-08-07, user ruling; supersedes the two-beat develop, which shipped from Round 8 until now). A picture that lands mid-session simply appears, exactly as one that mounted with its surface already does. Cause on record, in the user's words: "it doesn't feel necessary and would just be another thing that needs to configure for other clients/themes." The grayscale→ink chemistry was a per-engagement judgement call wearing the clothes of a default — every reskin would have had to form an opinion about it, and it bought too little to be worth that. The removal takes the `animateIn` and `delay` props, the mount-time refs that fed them (`printUrlAtMount`, `sheetAtMount`, `hadPrintAtMount`), and the sheet's develop stagger with it; `PrintReveal` is now a plain `<img>` carrying only the object-fit and drag contract, which is the whole reason it stays a component. **What this does NOT touch: the gray STATES.** A superseded frame ghosting under a re-picture, and an unchosen sheet sitting washed, are information rather than decoration — they say *this is being replaced* and *nothing is chosen yet* — so they stay, on the house tokens. The general form worth carrying: an animation that has to be re-judged per engagement is a liability; a state that encodes a fact is not. **Retained from the old ruling, still true wherever a slow reveal is used:** a long reveal on the arrival EASE alone front-loads ~75% of the change into the first quarter and reads as a crossfade — never run a slow reveal on EASE by itself.
4. **No springs on paper; no Tailwind default curves in the print system.** The expanded card mounts on EASE/DUR.beat and exits on EASE_EXIT/DUR.cut (the 280/28 spring bounced); the re-develop ghost dims on the tokens (`transition-all duration-500` killed); the photo-box caption lands on BEAT.hero.
5. **The manuscript is never unsaved.** The open card autosaves as you type (the CoachTakeover pattern: 700ms debounce, "Saved / Saving…" slug, flush on close and on idea navigation); the primary action is "Done", and click-out/Esc discard nothing. The only guarded write: a focus-only visit to an auto-named title must not persist the cleared name.
6. **Frame choosing is a room conversation** (user ruling): the Stage keeps the full contact-sheet choosing UI in presentation mode — Use buttons, loupe commit, identical to the team board. "Maybe this other one's better" said out loud IS the product's philosophy; no read-only sheet state exists.

## Round 7 — Stage craft pass (settled 2026-07-30, review pass)

1. **Navigation never carries the Kruger.** The Kruger marks the room's current object — the presenter, the leading idea, the winning lot — never chrome. Active nav on the dark register is white type over a 3px white underline; inactive sits at 0.45. (The pillar tabs had red floods, putting two Kruger bars on every results screen.)
2. **One primary per control-strip state.** Two red buttons side by side is a hierarchy failure, not emphasis. When returns exist for the pillar, "Show the returns" is the sole red action and "Open the ballot" recedes to secondary.
3. **The control strip mirrors the screen.** Whatever view the room sees is what the strip describes and can act on — a "Ready" strip with no actions over a live presenting wall is a dead first ten seconds. The cold-load view override feeds the strip an effective presenting state until the first real phase push.
4. **Peak #1 choreography.** The returns land from the bottom of the field, leader last (0.15s steps, capped near 1.2s), and the china-marker circles the leading idea's name once — the room's red pencil declares the winner. The mark and the leader's red vote slab are the only red on the working area.
5. **No colored display type on dark.** Team platform names set in team hue on the dark ground (cobalt at ~2:1, red running caps) fail the projector rule; they are white Sans Bold caps. Team hue lives in spines, tints, and swatches. Idea titles are Sans Bold on every Stage view (shortlist rows had drifted to 22–24px serif).
6. Config note: Hathaway's cobalt is `#2438D6` per this doc's own palette ("brightened from heritage #24298F for dark ground") — the config had drifted back to the heritage value.

## Round 6 — Board craft pass (settled 2026-07-29, review pass)

1. **The china-marker is wax, not paint.** On light registers the stroke renders translucent — `mix-blend-multiply`, opacity 0.55–0.65, stroke ≤2.5 when the mark spans a card frame — so the type under it stays legible. An opaque mark across an idea reads as rejection, not selection; the shortlisted card must stay the most readable frame on the sheet, not the least.

Red on the Board belongs to marks, stamps, and the LIVE chip only.
4. ~~**Control slots recede; prints dominate.** The Add/Scout dashed slots are pockets, not empty frames: 1.5px dash at 0.22 alpha with a 2% ink ground, labels at working size (15–16px).~~ **SUPERSEDED by Round 15 item 2 (2026-08-03):** the pockets no longer sit IN the masonry sequence. Add and Scout keep the same recessed dash treatment and the same 170px size, but they now occupy their own band between the category tabs and the grid, so an IDEA rather than a control is the first frame in column one. (Amended by Round 16 item 4: WHICH number that frame wears is the idea's own, not the wall's.) ## Round 14 — The beat is the work (settled 2026-08-01, user ruling)

The coach's considering beat is NEVER padding. User: "the thinking beat is the natural latency anyway right? we don't need to ADD more time to that." Correct — in a live deployment the pause IS the model composing, so the generation call goes in `fetchReply()` and the beat lasts exactly as long as it takes. The only surviving timer is `MIN_BEAT_MS` (450ms), a FLOOR so a fast reply doesn't flash the reserved page; a reply slower than the floor waits zero extra milliseconds. `SHOWCASE_BEAT_MS` stands in for generation here only because the scripted replies return instantly. Structure it this way, not as "replace this line with the model call" — that instruction gets followed by stacking a timer on top of a real await.

## Round 4 — Motion grammar + serif law (settled 2026-07-29, review pass)

1. **One motion grammar, tokenized in `app/lib/motion.ts`** — EASE `[0.16,1,0.3,1]` for arrivals, EASE_EXIT for departures, durations cut 0.25 / beat 0.5 / draw 0.55 / settle 1.4, stagger 0.08 (dense 0.04), page beats structure 0.10 → hero 0.28 → content 0.50 → detail 0.66. Seams draw from an origin and settle to a 0.25-opacity hairline; nothing wipes the screen. New transitions consume these tokens, never inline curves. The CoachTakeover arrival is the reference; the portal zoom is the only travel metaphor — curtains are dead.
2. **The serif law.** Ogilvy Serif earns its place at ≥28px, as a named moment (headline, numeral, masthead), one to two per surface. Below 24px — and on anything a participant edits, taps, or scans — it's Sans. Serif italic is reserved for actual quotation (D.O. lines, pulled quotes), never helper text. Idea titles are Sans Bold on every surface. Cause on record: 35% of serif call sites sat ≤22px, half italic, eroding on #0A0A0C next to same-size Sans — that's what "jarring" was.
3. **Rituals sit above the wire**: full-screen moments (unlock, launch takeover, coach takeover) live at z-10500+; the ticker never crawls over a ritual. It stays visible on working surfaces — that's its job.

## Round 2 — The Ogilvy Ethos layer (settled 2026-07-27, after user review)

User verdicts on v1: instructional meta-copy is jarring — fill in a REAL workshop; surfaces feel flat — add ambience; beyond the serif, nothing feels native to Ogilvy. The corrections:

### The content universe: The Divine Discontent Session
Ogilvy runs Basecamp on itself. Premise: one day, three teams, the agency out-inventing its own next chapter. Categories: **New Craft** (how the work gets made in the AI decade) / **Client Gravity** (what makes clients need the epicenter) / **Creative Culture** (how creative people grow here). Team creative platforms: **Sell or Else** / **The Well-Informed Unconscious** / **The Red Thread**. All boards, coaching, votes, briefs, ticker, and the Edition carry real session content — the platform explains itself through a believable day, not through captions. Instructional voice survives ONLY in the onboarding guide, empty states, and helper lines.

### The ambience system (each element native, none generic)
1. **Tartan** — the 2021 Ogilvy 485 heritage pattern, rebuilt as a tonal SVG (red/ink). Used as thin ambient bands and low-opacity fields on dark surfaces. Static.
2. **Type-as-texture** — oversized cropped Ogilvy Serif letterforms (the ligature story) at very low contrast behind dark-surface heroes. The identity IS the letterforms; use them at poster scale.
3. **Halftone materiality** — fine print-dot texture at 2–3% on dark grounds and on flood bands; the material truth of ad reproduction (replaces generic noise).
4. **Horizon warmth** — a soft red radial bloom low on dark surfaces (stage light, not decoration). Subtle; never behind body text.
5. **Red-on-pink signature** — #EB3F43 on #F5BAC5 for exactly two marquee moments: the guide overlay and the coaching intro. Pink is warmth, still not wallpaper.
6. **The highlighter bar** — flat color bar behind a key phrase (red default; blue/chartreuse allowed on print register). At most one per surface.
Motion rule, amended: static ambience everywhere EXCEPT the shader field (item 7), which is the sanctioned exception. The wire remains the only other moving element.
7. **The shader field** (ShaderGradient, brand presets ember/blush) — slow atmospheric motion, allowed ONLY on landing, team select, stage-idle, and the coaching intro overlay. Never on working/facilitation states, never behind body text without a scrim, always lazy-loaded. Tartan bands live on theater-dark surfaces only — never on light or print registers (settled by round-2 review: the print masthead keeps its crisp double rule).
